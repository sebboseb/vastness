# Core fantasy

The player can shape reality through intention. Green Lantern and dream references describe agency: an intention can become a place, object or possibility. They do not prescribe a visual design or franchise aesthetic.

# Emotional target

Mystery, adventure and anticipation at each threshold, with enough spatial coherence to feel present and able to explore. Like an autogenerated ASCII city, incomplete representation and ambiguity should suggest possibility: literally anything might appear next.

# Current art-direction hypotheses

- Abstract representation may make generative inconsistency feel intentional and intriguing.
- Stylized low-detail geometry and simplified color/value structure may preserve readable paths while leaving detail to imagination.
- Graphic or painterly surfaces may unite mismatched generated forms through a shared visual language.
- Procedural marks, glyph-like rendering and non-photorealistic splat treatments may evoke an unfinished world that responds to intention.
- Compare treatments against the same spatial scene before choosing a branch to investigate. These are hypotheses, not a final style.

# Things to avoid

Photorealism as the default quality target; weirdness that reads only as failed realism; literal Green Lantern styling; a fixed dream aesthetic; clutter that obscures walkable space; premature gameplay systems, UI polish or permanent style commitments. Keep experiments separate from M0.

# North-star sentence

“Anything could be beyond the next threshold, and the world can become what I intend.”
